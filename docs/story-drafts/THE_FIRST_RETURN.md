# The First Return

Original main-story continuation after the Hollow Constable encounter. Working manuscript for playable integration; no optional route or personal chapter is included in this count.

The wheel had stopped. The gate was still shut.

For several breaths nobody moved toward it. A loose end of wire tapped the watch captain's sleeve. When the wind turned, it tapped again.

“Bram?” Senn called from the other side.

“Here.”

“Can I move the cart?”

Tamsin crouched beside the lever. The captain's hand lay across its base. She shifted the hand clear with the folded edge of his coat, then looked into the slot beneath it.

“Not yet. There's a tooth caught in the return.”

She held out her palm. Hux gave her the flattened cap.

“I was using that,” he said.

“You'll get it back bent.”

“That would be an improvement.”

He went to the gate before she could answer. On the other side, Senn had both hands on the cart again.

Mara watched Tamsin work. The captain's cuff had ridden up when she moved his hand. A pale line crossed his wrist beneath the grime, where a watch strap had once kept the weather off.

She had the same line.

“Did you know him?” she asked Senn.

“Enough to ask him to open this yesterday.”

Mara waited.

“He wanted an admission stamp for the cart. I told him I was taking people out, not bringing them in.”

“What did he say?”

“He asked for it again.”

The answer was so ordinary that Mara looked back at the lever. It was easier to look at a mechanism that had gone wrong.

“Watch freight passed through,” Senn added. “I saw the sacks. Don't write down that the gate wouldn't open.”

Vale, who had taken out their sheet, stopped with the pencil above it.

“May I keep that account?”

“That part. Ask before you add where I was going.”

They drew a short line under the three copied names and began a separate paragraph.

The tooth came free with a noise like a dropped spoon. Tamsin stood and pulled the lever upward. The wheel moved a little, then caught.

“Bram, take the weight. Hux, when the leaf opens, hold it there. Don't let it run.”

Bram eased his shield away from the jamb. Hux caught the gate with both hands. For a moment the opening narrowed; then the wheel completed its turn and daylight reached the groove where the cart had stuck.

“Now,” Bram said. “Lift your wheel.”

Senn lifted. The barefoot man stooped beside them despite Iona's warning and put his shoulder under the cart rail. They raised it together. The wheel cleared the stone with an ugly scraping sound.

“That heel will need looking at again,” Iona said.

“It was attached to the rest of me,” he replied. “The rest had a job.”

“So did the bandage.”

He glanced down. A red spot was spreading through it.

“Then we can both apologize at the shed.”

Iona took the folded coat from under his arm. He let her. She put it over his shoulders before taking his elbow.

The two walkers followed: a woman carrying an empty birdcage and a boy with a bundle tied in a flour sack. When the boy reached the threshold, he stepped back to let the woman pass first. She caught his sleeve and brought him alongside her.

Hux moved to the rear of the cart as promised. Senn tested the brake before trusting him with the slope. The repaired arm held for one turn of the wheel, slipped, and caught again.

“You said it wouldn't hold by itself.”

“Still true.”

“Good. I can work with true.”

They took the cart down the short incline. The woman under the left side of the canvas pushed the cover back. A square of embroidery sat in her lap, its needle fixed through the cloth.

“My box is under the seat,” she told Hux. “If you tip us, catch that first.”

“And you?”

“I can shout.”

The other passenger made a small sound. Hux thought it was a cough until the woman began to laugh with him.

Renn came up from the bend with two people carrying a door between them. They had taken its handle off so it would lie flat. Mara stepped aside to let them through.

“For the shed,” Renn said. “We can put it over the crates. Keeps a blanket off the wet floor.”

“Who gave it to you?”

“The woman whose roof you volunteered.”

Mara looked toward the customs building.

“She wants someone to fetch her husband from the water steps,” Renn continued. “She'll come tell you which one. Please let her finish the description.”

“I will.”

Renn watched her for another moment, then helped carry the door onward.

Sable remained beside the wheel. There was a brass plate below it, partly hidden by the captain's coat. She pulled the coat clear without turning the body over.

EVACUATION COMPLETE.

The letters were filled with black enamel. There were two screw holes above them and two below. At the lower right, a fresh burr marked where a tool had slipped.

Mara took the folded instruction from her pocket. The same words, the same clean stamps. Paper outside; brass inside.

“A copy?” she asked.

“At least two objects saying the same thing,” Sable said. “I haven't earned the rest yet.”

She touched neither the plate nor the wire.

“And the bell?”

“The wheel stopped. The bell hasn't. That gives us something to test.”

Beyond the passage, a note sounded over the roofs. Mara counted its strokes. This time the lever stayed where Tamsin had left it.

Tamsin put the bent cap into her hand.

“For Hux. Tell him I owe him another.”

“What keeps the gate open now?”

“This wedge until the next person takes it out. I'll mark it. Then someone has to look at it when they pass. There isn't a repair that replaces that.”

She took a piece of yellow chalk from her pocket and marked the wedge and the stone above it with matching bars.

Bram glanced at the captain. “He's in the return path.”

Mara knelt and folded the coat across his chest. Together they moved him into the watch shelter, clear of the threshold. She checked his pockets for an identification card and found a damp tobacco pouch, a key and a folded blank form. The key stayed on the bench beside him. Vale recorded where they had put it.

The captain had asked twice for a stamp he had not required of freight. He had also carried a blank form in his pocket. Neither fact explained the other.

Mara left them both in the account.

At the shed, the barefoot man had been given a crate to sit on. The woman with the embroidery was telling a stranger how to fit a blanket around the door's exposed hinge.

“Evi Pell,” she said when Iona asked her name. “This is Jorren Saye. He'd tell you himself if you stopped asking him whether he can speak.”

Jorren lifted two fingers from the blanket in greeting.

Iona nodded to him. “Would you prefer questions you can answer that way?”

He lifted one finger.

“One for yes?”

One finger again.

Evi returned to the blanket. Iona waited for Jorren's answer before moving it.

Senn had backed the empty cart against the shed wall. Hux was trying the brake while they watched. The first jar of water was already below its shoulder.

“We need a wheel chock,” Senn said. “And something to cover that wire. It will cut the next hand.”

Hux looked around the yard. A short piece of firewood lay under the customs step. He asked the shed owner before taking it.

Renn turned the signal board toward Mara. Five marks occupied the space beneath SHED. Below them were the words: ONE PLACE LEFT. ASK HERE BEFORE NEXT CART.

“You didn't count Senn,” Mara said.

“Senn told me they're going back.”

Mara looked at the porter. They were wrapping the brake wire with a strip of discarded canvas.

“Do you want an escort?”

“To the next passage. After that I know the ward. I'll bring the cart back here if the road stays usable.”

“It will,” Mara began.

Renn's pencil stopped.

“We'll check it,” Mara said. “And send word if it changes.”

Renn finished the mark they had been making.

Senn drew three short routes on the back of a customs form. One reached the triage passage, where people were waiting. One went under the registry arch, where the names on the wall could be checked against the missing lists. The third passed the workshop wire that still carried the bell's instruction deeper into the cordon.

“I can show you the turn for each,” they said. “I can't take the cart down all three.”

Vale folded their account with the copied names facing inward. Mara kept the paper instruction in a separate pocket. Hux brought the cart forward and tested the covered brake once more.

Mara went back to Renn before following them.

“I haven't found who sent it.”

“I can see that.”

“There is a brass copy inside. Same wording. I left it where it was.”

Renn wrote that beneath the question on the board. There was room for an answer, when they had one.
