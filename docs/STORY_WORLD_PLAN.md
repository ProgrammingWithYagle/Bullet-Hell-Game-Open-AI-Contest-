# Grave Accord: long-form story and cast plan

This is an authoring plan, not a claim that these chapters are playable. The user has approved a cooperative main campaign, optional character chapters for all seven survivors, deep worldbuilding, and deliberate development of side characters and antagonists. Their latest ambition is **five million words in the base story**, excluding optional routes and character chapters. Only the user decides when the game is finished.

The existing foundation is The Open Ledger: one fifteen-encounter arc, three chapters, fourteen decisions and three endings. Seven survivor letters are epilogues, not seven completed character arcs. Existing English, Spanish and Arabic prose deliveries do not constitute the long-form campaign. The running reviews and implementation ledger remain the authority for shipped behavior.

## Scale and structure

Treat five million words as the long-term English main-story target. Count narrative content, not UI, guides, code, translated copies, repeated transcripts, or optional character/side-route text. Track authored main-story words separately from the words encountered on one playthrough. Before claiming a length milestone, state which measure it uses. Do not enlarge the count by replaying the same dialogue or counting alternative translations.

Use complete volumes, each containing several chapters and shorter scenes. Each volume needs its own dramatic question, changes in relationships, payoff and a satisfying stopping point. The existing three-chapter Open Ledger arc is a starting installment, not a template that every future volume must repeat. Individual chapters may be investigations, journeys, negotiations, rescues, quiet recovery or confrontations; combat encounters follow the dramatic need. The number of volumes and chapters is not fixed yet. Do not fill a volume to a quota after its story has ended.

Main campaign remains finishable with any class, solo or cooperatively. Character chapters develop the focal survivor and can include tailored challenges or optional true solo duels. Cooperative versions involve the whole party. Players should see the chapter's class or solo requirements before starting a dedicated challenge. Preserve support-class usefulness and the established difficulty rules. Do not force a multiplayer party to wait through another player's private fight.

At a hypothetical 200–250 words/minute, five million main-story words mean about 333–417 reading hours before combat and other interaction. This is arithmetic with an assumed pace, not a measured playtime. The earlier whole-game estimate of 500–1,000 focused hours is superseded by this much larger narrative scope. A new production forecast needs a completed-volume sample with drafting, revision, continuity, implementation and review effort recorded.

## Established world facts

- The city is Lorn. Its council announced evacuation complete while people remained behind the cordon. The opening gate, fresh chalk names and alternate accounts are established evidence.
- Eleven bells copy civic instructions. A twelfth has no name. The first arc establishes that the final order identifies whom the dead should ignore; it does not establish the origin of every death or prove that the epidemic was invented.
- Dead workers, civic machinery, animal-like creatures and household objects preserve different routines. Do not automatically give every creature a human biography or every machine a human voice.
- The infirmary, foundry, river gates, railway, workshops, chapel, registry and watch are institutions with material needs and competing internal factions. Show who cooks, repairs, transports, pays, waits and decides.
- Shelter, Witness and Defiance are three existing outcomes. None is the sole true ending. Later stories must honor the chosen civic consequences without reducing the other paths to failures.
- The seven letters depict possible ordinary lives after struggle. Preserve their hopeful specificity. They do not erase responsibility or settle every relationship.

Maintain a chronology with confirmed events, testimony, institutional claims and unresolved questions in separate fields. A character may be mistaken. The author should know which facts are uncertain and why. Do not reveal the secret Uncounted material merely to explain an early chapter.

## Ensemble writing standard

Give every recurring person a purpose beyond explaining the protagonist. Track their immediate want, longer need, capability, limitation, relationships, choices and consequences. Larger roles can have more page time; smaller roles still need deliberate characterization and a life outside the player's visit. Emotional importance, moral importance and boss threat grade are separate.

Balance the seven survivor arcs by dramatic completeness. Each needs a developed starting position, sustained pressure, consequential decisions, relationships that change and a credible aftermath. Rotate who notices, leads, makes mistakes, disagrees and receives help. Do not let one survivor solve the others' personal stories. Equal care does not require every scene to include all seven speakers.

For side characters, plan recurrence and independent agency. Some can refuse help, criticize the party, keep private information, or make a decision the party dislikes. A rescued person should not disappear after thanking the heroes. Communities need disagreements and mundane activity as well as suffering.

For antagonists, distinguish deliberate beneficiaries, opportunists, ideological opponents, compromised protectors, involuntary echoes and nonhuman threats. Understanding a motive does not excuse an act. Avoid making every boss a noble worker who once disobeyed the council. A boss's visible mechanics should illuminate its habits, decisions or effect on the world; a biography alone is insufficient development.

Write concrete dialogue with differing vocabularies, priorities and rhythms. Let people misunderstand, evade, interrupt, joke, bargain and change their minds. Preserve quiet scenes and ordinary pleasures. Horror should have consequences beyond a decorative corpse. Avoid repeated speeches about names, doors and ledgers when a scene can convey its meaning through a new action.

## Seven survivor arc treatments

These are working treatments, not finished scripts. Three episode beats below describe an arc shape; they are not a mandatory three-chapter limit.

### Mara Rook — Soldier — The Empty Line

Mara knows how to make an order executable. She must learn how to make authority answerable to the people who bear its cost. Her competence should remain useful while her old habits become a source of conflict. Her first instinct is to gather everyone into a legible plan; a survivor's refusal is not simply another obstacle to clear.

Begin with a watch instruction whose purpose changed as it passed between offices. Bring her into conflict with a former colleague and witnesses who remember her help differently. End with a decision about who gets to set the next rules, followed by evidence of whether she can obey a limit she did not write. The Hollow Constable offers a distorted mirror, not an automatic excuse that orders remove responsibility. Her letter, One More Place, is a possible human payoff.

### Iona Vale — Medic — The Unfinished Chart

Iona begins with an immediate, useful promise: start with the person who needs care. The arc should test the burden of believing that every outcome is hers to carry. Patients and porters have expertise and preferences of their own. Care must involve consent, resources and shared work as well as courage.

Begin with an admission record that fails to describe the person in front of her. Continue through a ward whose residents disagree about her proposed rescue. Resolve a conflict in which accepting another person's choice changes her understanding of responsibility. Use the Matron and Glass Surgeon as distinct interlocutors; neither should become a second copy of Iona's dilemma. Clean Bandages gives an ordinary-life horizon. Neris Vale's surname does not by itself establish a family relationship.

### Hux Bell — Heavy — The Quiet Shift

Hux can keep a machine or firing line working after others have stopped. His story asks who benefits from that endurance and whether being indispensable has become a way to avoid asking for help. His class's force and rhythm should coexist with patience and craft.

Begin among workers who know him outside the party's heroic reputation. Continue through a production bargain that helps one group while shifting the burden onto another. End with a concrete change in who can stop, repair or govern a machine. The Cinder Foreman can defend exploitative production deliberately; Edda Marr offers a different response to scarcity. Preserve the wooden horse and shared switch in The Quiet Shift as earned possibilities, not instant cures for labor conflict.

### Bram Voss — Tank — A Door on Hinges

Bram's protection can become control if he always decides where safety ends. His arc should give the people behind his shield a voice and make his patience as important as his ability to endure damage.

Begin with conflicting meanings of the red door. Continue with someone who chooses to leave a place Bram believes defensible. Resolve a confrontation in which keeping an exit open costs him a certainty he values. The Chain Marshal and Gate Widow need their own positions and histories, rather than serving as anonymous tests of his goodness. His quiet work on a domestic door can complete an argument that began in a battle.

### Sable Quill — Mage — A Useful Margin

Sable can interpret systems that others find frightening. Her danger is treating explanation as permission. The story should test her habits of evidence, her relationship to expertise, and her ability to learn from people who use a different vocabulary.

Begin with a small inconsistency in a bell's instruction that a nonexpert noticed first. Continue through testimony she cannot safely reduce to a neat theory. Resolve a choice about who can understand, challenge and change the knowledge she preserves. The Choir, Red Abbess and Unlit Astronomer must offer different arguments. Keep the twelfth bell's established mystery coherent. Teaching someone to write across a theorem is a relationship payoff, not merely a metaphor repeated from the main story.

### Tamsin Reed — Engineer — Borrowed Parts

Tamsin knows how to get a broken system running. Her arc asks who will maintain it, who can inspect it and who bears the risk when she is gone. Her ingenuity should create real benefits and real obligations.

Begin with a repair whose original operators understand a constraint she missed. Continue through an apparently elegant solution that makes one district dependent on another. Resolve a conflict about ownership, access and the right to shut a system down. Ressa Noll's pump house and the Mortuary Engine connect personal choices to infrastructure. The seven signatures in Borrowed Parts should follow shared work, disagreement and repair.

### Vale Ash — Assassin — Return Address

Vale is practiced at passing unseen and carrying information. Their story should explore the difference between protecting a person's privacy and deciding on their behalf that the truth is too dangerous. Their restraint and attention deserve as much dramatic space as deception or violence.

Begin with the undelivered letter and a request to find someone without exposing them. Continue through witnesses who want incompatible uses made of the same record. Resolve a decision about what Vale may reveal, to whom, and with what right of reply. The Courier, Ink Notary, Memory Taxer and Stationmaster each touch communication differently. Preserve the small blue ribbon as a specific response from another person; do not turn it into a universal secret-conspiracy token.

## Recurring side-character working cast

These five additions are proposals for forthcoming scripts, not people already present in the playable reader. They need their own continuity records before publication.

- **Senn Arlow, ward porter:** knows transport, supply and the ward's social arrangements. Wants care work to remain sustainable. Can disagree with Iona's rescue plan and still care deeply about the patients. Give Senn a consequential decision beyond assisting a survivor.
- **Miri Fen, copyist:** wants testimony preserved without making vulnerable witnesses easy to locate. Has previously accepted paid alterations. Their relationship with Vale and Sable should involve both cooperation and accountability. Privacy is a material concern, not a twist used to withhold convenient exposition.
- **Olan Pike, foundry cook:** measures policies in portions and shifts. Can challenge Hux and Tamsin over the cost of heroic repairs. Give Olan loyalties, compromises and relationships among workers instead of making the kitchen an unlimited supply point.
- **Dena Yarrow, chapel caretaker:** has a living practice of faith distinct from the Abbess's authority. Wants the chapel to remain useful to people who disagree about belief. Can oppose a leader without abandoning the people who remain there.
- **Renn Tallow, outer-watch signal runner:** has worked with Mara and fears what an open cordon may bring to people outside it. Their competence and fear can produce real opposition. Let evidence and consequences change the relationship over time; avoid resolving it with one persuasive speech.

## Antagonist treatment priorities

Every boss belongs in the cast register, including nonhuman and object encounters. The first treatment group should demonstrate different approaches before extending the same standard across the whole roster.

- **Hollow Constable:** investigate the choices made while alive as well as the instruction repeated after death. Give witnesses room to disagree about the captain. Mara cannot restore legitimacy merely by issuing a kinder command.
- **Ash Baker / Edda Marr:** retain her second account of portions instead of debts. Show the practical conflicts around scarce food through people who depended on the ovens. The aftermath should leave a question about feeding the next shift, not only a defeated body.
- **Sluice Keeper / Ressa Noll:** connect the gates to people on both banks. Establish what her relief decision did through evidence and differing testimony. Do not casually transfer the Regent's responsibility onto her.
- **Stationmaster / Neris Vale:** keep the missing arrival unresolved until a scene earns the answer. Individual passengers and staff can have differing aims. Shared surnames do not automatically imply kinship.
- **Cinder Foreman:** develop a deliberate beneficiary of exploitation. Their argument should arise from power and choices, not an interchangeable tragic final shift.
- **Red Abbess:** separate institutional authority, belief, service and complicity. Give people who stayed at the chapel independent reasons and different judgments.
- **Mourning Kettle:** develop place, routine and human relationships around a nonhuman encounter. Sound, objects and behavior can carry its story; it need not deliver a human villain's monologue.
- **The Uncounted:** preserve secrecy and the first arc's existing meaning. A secret encounter must not become compulsory to understand or finish the main plot.

Future treatments must include motives or behavioral purpose, connections outside the protagonist, evidence before confrontation, the encounter's dramatic function and consequences afterward. Some bosses may have larger arcs and recur. Some may be brief; their brevity is not permission for generic filler.

## Long-form implementation work

The current reader and fifteen-fight route are not yet a multi-volume engine. Add stable volume/chapter/scene identities, lazy narrative loading, compact persistent reading cursors and resumable chapter boundaries. Keep reading position separate from earned completion and reward receipts. A long story cannot depend on an uninterrupted room connection or ephemeral hosted SQLite.

Cooperative rooms will need clear chapter/version information and spoiler-aware admission alongside Story/Free Play categories. Reading remains untimed and explicitly ready-controlled. Personal menus do not pause the whole room. Preserve offline export/import, localization ownership, typed names and the separation of modified/practice runs from records.

Track narrative quality through scene review and continuity checks, not word count or passing code tests alone. For each completed installment, review voice, causality, character agency, pacing, repetition, route consequences and emotional payoff. Software can check IDs and references; it cannot certify compelling writing.
