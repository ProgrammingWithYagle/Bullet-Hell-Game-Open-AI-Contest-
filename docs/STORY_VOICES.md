# Narration and character voices

The user found the campaign dialogue too uniform and asked for narration to carry descriptive detail. This revision addresses a concrete attribution issue:the chapter, report and ending prose had a focal survivor's name displayed as if the entire paragraph were spoken aloud. It now has explicit narration semantics and no speaking heading. The focal portrait remains an illustration, separate from who is speaking. The actual added dialogue turns retain the correct named survivor and language direction.

Forty currently used short turns are revised in each English, Spanish and Arabic edition:chapter conversations, reports1–14and the three endings. Superseded short prologue/report0text is not counted as a new delivery. The two longer gate/first-return manuscripts remain unchanged. The checked authored playable main-story count is5,058English words across104unique passages; the opening remains3,027. Translations, repeated archive/transcript displays, optional letters, UI and unimplemented manuscripts are excluded. These are authored counts, not one-playthrough length or literary acceptance.

Mara's tendency to organize appears with practical oversights and an admission of habitual signing. Hux notices material problems and uses dry humor. Tamsin wants others to respect the limits of a mechanism while she works. Sable can read something incorrectly and ask to try again. Bram has bodily limits and uncomfortable memories. Iona asks whose practical knowledge she should use. Vale can admit a missed delivery and speak directly. These are starting distinctions, not compulsory catchphrases or fully developed arcs.

The technical distinction is explicit in StoryLine.kind and the reader's page semantics. Descriptive text is narration, spoken turns are dialogue, and a portrait does not supply a voice. Bookmarks ignore a new semantic tag alone while retaining text/speaker/focal changes in their content stamp. Actual controller checks verify heading changes, Arabic direction and saved positions. Shared gameplay and content revision remain unchanged.

This is still a limited revision of a prototype installment. Longer scene construction, side-character recurrence, living antagonists, boss character arcs and the new twelve-class ensemble need continued writing. Human/native-language/literary/pacing review remains open. There is no new character audio or expression/action sprite set in this pass. ## Perrin and Heda working voices

Perrin Moss can be exact about a result and evasive about an ordinary personal question. Their dyehouse experience gives them useful competence before their recent Alchemist training; do not turn every interaction into a lesson in failure. They have tastes, family connections and a capacity for dry humor. Narration supplies practical detail. Spoken lines need not explain recipe rules, rank systems or the story's themes.

Heda Linn checks incoming ward stores and knew Perrin at the dyehouse. She practised as an Alchemist before Perrin entered training, but no exact grade is established. She wants her kettle open, her work checked and an answer to a family question. She can tease Perrin without becoming a mouthpiece for the correct interpretation of every event. Neither introduction completes an arc. Future expressions/actions/voices need separate production and review.
