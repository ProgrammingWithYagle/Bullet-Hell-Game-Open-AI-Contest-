# Encounter design: techniques come from the boss

The user’s latest direction is that each boss must rely on its own themed techniques. Not every boss should spam projectiles. These criteria guide the remaining original-quality passes and new roster identities; they do not claim existing playable encounters are missing or finally accepted.

- State the boss’s theme as actions that change player decisions. Alchemist aims, mixes, distills, cools, couples and catalyzes. A harmless shape without a useful response is not enough.
- Make each later phase add or change a technique. Counts, speed and health alone do not establish phase identity. Show its mechanism promptly and announce every dangerous change.
- Give Brutal and Crazed themed interactions, not only denser emissions. Projectile multipliers apply to projectile-bearing attacks; a boss may instead use movement, terrain, pressure, bodies, tools, timing or transformations.
- Give beneficial states a visible cause and practical consequence. Show vapor opening at coolant and a pressure gauge falling when a vessel cools. Preserve alternative counterplay such as crossing a warned bank with a dash.
- Build readable warnings from geometry, silhouettes, motion and sound. Color is reinforced by shape. Development guides remain manually available; public guidance is optional Baby-only. Final learning should not depend on a wall of automatic text.
- Animate locomotion with actual drawn poses. Retain requests, native returns, source ownership, whole-frame normalization and independent packing evidence. Do not describe an idle sprite translating as a completed walk cycle.
- Verify actual authority, body/arena geometry, warning locks, prediction where applicable, cleanup, multiplayer replication and every map/difficulty/form. Separate automatic stationary samples from deliberate action witnesses; neither is a personally played clear or final balance acceptance.

Normal remains appropriate to the fixed threat grade. Global difficulty, form, assistance/revival rules, and final human review are separate. Keep one progress ledger, source-linked changelogs and honest release gates.
