# Twelve shared disciplines

Accepted roster: the existing Soldier, Medic, Heavy, Tank, Mage, Engineer and Assassin, plus **Swordsman, Summoner, Ranger, Alchemist and Bard**. All five additions are accepted scope. The current playable build has eight classes, including Bard. Its first shared kit and Story foundation are implemented; Swordsman, Summoner, Ranger and Alchemist remain unfinished. No completed twelve-class roster or final class acceptance is claimed.

Classes are disciplines practiced by many people. The seven established leads recently entered theirs; practical experience from their previous lives does not make them veteran masters of that class. New disciplines need multiple practitioners across the world, including teachers, novices, rivals and antagonists. A class identity must be separate from a character ID, portrait, moral position or fixed personality.

## Bard direction

The user particularly likes Bard and associates it with the Fool. Keep the appeal of performance, surprise and layered identity as a broad creative stimulus while developing original rules and a distinct story. Do not borrow a named pathway, sequence system, distinctive powers, rituals, characters or reveal structure from Lord of Mysteries.

An original working core is a prepared phrase with a stated reach and duration. A player builds cadence through useful combat/support actions, then chooses when to resolve it. Different phrases might steady allies, displace a marked threat or produce a narrow resonant strike. Silence, interruption, distance and timing provide counterplay. These are proposals, not implemented abilities or accepted final balance.

The city does not belong to Bards simply because it has bells. Mechanics should distinguish human performance, civic signal machinery, supernatural resonance and what characters merely believe about them. Other classes and civilians retain important ways to investigate or change the bell network. A Bard can be shy, blunt or indifferent to fame; the class is not a compulsory theatrical personality.

## Integration order

First establish real ordinary-enemy objectives and Story mastery foundations, then build the first complete new class through the same authority/input/render/persistence boundaries. Swordsman is a useful melee test; Bard is a strong narrative and support test. Choose the first implementation based on the shared systems ready at that point, without treating the other accepted classes as optional again.

Each addition needs its own base kit, weapons, specialization behavior and actual D- through SS+ rules, mastery paths, difficulty/support viability, solo/co-op coverage, localization, art/audio references, readable counters, profile/save compatibility and both Story/Free Play availability. The existing54nodes-per-class arrangement is a current shared content model, not a substitute for designing genuine new techniques. Full twelve-class balance and performance remain unfinished.

Summoner particularly needs deterministic unit budgets and ownership, pathfinding, target selection, damage credit, cleanup and late/reconnecting-player behavior. No unit should remain after its owner or encounter has ended. Commanding an army should remain useful when party size grows, without filling the entire screen with particles or hiding enemy warnings.

The story should introduce disciplines through people and tasks before explaining their advanced possibilities. The larger ensemble and human antagonists should include differing practitioners of the same discipline rather than one representative character per class.
